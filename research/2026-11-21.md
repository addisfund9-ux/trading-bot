## Ideas

### 1. "Stops left today" after every loss
- **Area:** risk · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** After each loss the guard says how many planned stops fit before the daily limit (e.g. 5 USDT room ÷ 1 USDT risk = 5). Under 3, it says to halve the next trade's size; at 0 you're done. You see the end of the day coming in trades, not after it's gone.
- **Notes:** FXStreet's piece argues the useful number is the buffer measured in stops (room to the drawdown floor ÷ typical stop), checked before each session, and that size should drop when it's under 3. The bot already has `DAILY_LOSS_LIMIT` (give-back from the day's high, default 5) and `RISK_PER_TRADE_USD` (default 1) in bot.py. Build: in the guard's loss alert and in /tilt, add `stops_left = floor((DAILY_LOSS_LIMIT - giveback_today) / RISK_PER_TRADE)`; text "3 stops left today", "under 3 — next trade at half size (0.5 USDT risk)", "0 — daily stop reached". This differs from the 11 Nov "soft stop" (one warning stage in dollars): it's a running count tied to size. It never suggests trading more; at 0 it repeats the existing rest-day rule.

### 2. Planned risk vs real loss in /week
- **Area:** risk · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Your rules say risk about 1 USDT a trade, but leverage changes margin, not the loss at the stop. /week compares each losing trade with that 1 USDT: "losers averaged 2.3× planned risk, 4 lost over 3×". It shows if stops are skipped or size is set from leverage.
- **Notes:** Kraken and London Loves Business both give the same formula: size = (risk amount) ÷ (entry − stop distance), and leverage only decides margin. The 25 Oct session (leverage raised to 40×) is the pattern this catches: same stop habits, bigger loss per trade. Build: in review.py, for positions with `pnl < 0`, compute `ratio = -pnl / RISK_PER_TRADE`; /week adds one line with the average ratio, the count over 2× and over 3×, and the worst one (coin + time). Optionally split by leverage band (≤10×, 11–20×, >20×) to show where the overshoot happens. Uses imported trades only; no new API calls. Fees count in pnl, which is fine — they're part of the real loss.
