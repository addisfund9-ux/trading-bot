# Daily research — 2026-11-20

**Headline:** No API change affects the bot today, and only two scanner ideas held up: show how close each mover is to its 7-day high, and tag trades that fade a sharp 15-minute spike, because new research says that bounce is too small to pay your fees.

Today's focus: scanner and signal quality (does a top mover keep going or snap back?), plus the usual changelog check.

## Ideas

### 1. "Near the high" number on each scanner card
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** A 2023 study found that, in liquid coins, being close to the recent high predicted the next move better than the raw % gain. Show "0.6% under 7d high" on each card from data the scanner already pulls, and only add it to the score if backtest.py shows it helps.
- **Notes:** Fičura (2023, weekly data, CoinMarketCap 2017–2022) sorted coins by ln(close / k-week high). In "large and liquid" coins (cap ≥ $50M, weekly volume ≥ $5M) the top-vs-bottom quintile spread was +2.68%/week (t = 4.93), stronger than plain 1-week momentum (+1.36%/week, t = 2.33), and it held after BTC and 3-factor adjustment. In small or illiquid coins the same sorts reversed. The scanner's 20M USDT/day minimum puts every candidate well inside the "liquid" group. Caveats: weekly horizon, no trading costs, not perps; so treat it as a display column first, not a score input. Build: in scanner.py `analyse()`, the 1h klines (300 candles ≈ 12.5 days) already give `high`/`low`; add `hi7 = max(h1["high"][-168:])`, `lo7 = min(h1["low"][-168:])`, and `from_hi = (price / hi7 - 1) * 100` (for shorts, distance above `lo7`). Show it on the card and in /scan. Mirror in webapp/index.html. Then add the same field to backtest.py's replay and compare Clean signals with `from_hi` under 1% vs over 5% before giving it any points. It also gives a plain reality check next to "Stretched" (distance from the 1h EMA 20 in ATRs): stretched but at a fresh high is a different picture from stretched and already 8% off the high.

### 2. Tag "fade" trades and show their results in /week
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** medium
- **Why:** A 2026 study of Binance 15m candles found sharp, taker-driven moves do bounce a little, but the edge (~1.3 bp) is far below a 5 bp round trip and gone within 4h. Tag entries that go against the last big 15m candle so /week shows if fading spikes costs you.
- **Notes:** Kitron & Wengrowicz (arXiv, Aug 2026) used 15-minute candles on 183 high-volume Binance USDT spot pairs (Jan 2025 – Feb 2026, plus a held-out Feb–Aug 2026 test). The direction of the next bar flips slightly more often after big, aggressive taker-driven moves (AUC ~0.53), and the effect is real across venues, but "the gross edge peaks near 1.3 bp per trade against a 5 bp round-trip cost", and it fades out by 4 hours. The paper's summary also says it replicates on USDⓈ-M perps; that part couldn't be checked from the text read today. For a scalper at 10–40× paying taker fees, "it's spiked, it must come back" is mostly a feeling, not an edge. Fading is also what a flip looks like on a tilt day (long loses → short the same coin). Build: tradechart.py already fetches candles around each trade. For each imported position, get the 15m candle that closed before entry; if its body is over ~1.5× the 1h ATR (scanner's `atr()`), and the entry side is against that candle's direction, tag the trade `fade`. Store the tag in store.py next to the existing revenge/flip flags. /week adds one line: "Fade trades: 6 → −3.10 USDT, 33% win" next to the rest. No new rule or block at first; the point is to see your own numbers. Complements the 8 Nov "Wait a candle" flag (which warns against chasing a spike) by covering the opposite urge.
