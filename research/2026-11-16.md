## Ideas

### 1. Tilt guard reads every row on busy days
- **Area:** bot · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** The guard asks Binance for the last 26 hours of income in one call capped at 1000 rows. On a heavy overtrading day, partial fills and fees can pass that cap and the newest rows — the revenge trades it exists to catch — may be missed. Page by time until a call returns fewer than 1000.
- **Notes:** `guard.py` → `Client.income(start_ms)` calls `/fapi/v1/income` with `startTime` and `limit=1000`, and `check()` passes `now - 26h`. Each fill adds at least a REALIZED_PNL and a COMMISSION row, and funding adds more, so a day like 25 Oct with partial fills can get close. Binance removed the `page` parameter on this endpoint (changelog, 2026-10-15 entry, effective 16 Oct), so paging must be by time: loop while a response has 1000 rows, set the next `startTime` to the last row's `time + 1`, stop at 5 pages, and log a warning if the cap is still hit. This fits the 14 Nov idea of having the guard say when it stops working.

### 2. "Range used" flag on scanner cards
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Top movers are often already done for the day. From the 4h candles the scanner already loads, compare today's high–low with the coin's average daily range over 14 days. Over 100% shows a "Range used" flag, so a late chase looks as risky as it is.
- **Notes:** `scanner.analyse()` already loads 300 × 4h candles (about 50 days). Group them by UTC day (6 bars), take the mean of (high − low) / open over the last 14 full days, and divide today's range so far by it. No extra API calls. Show it as "Range 130%" on the card and in `webapp/index.html`, add a "Range used" flag over 100%, and record it with the picks so the evening scorecard can check later whether high-range picks followed through worse for you. This is different from "Stretched" (distance from the EMA) and "Late" (age of the EMA alignment): it measures how far the coin has already travelled today against its own normal day. Thresholds are a starting guess; tune them on your own picks.
