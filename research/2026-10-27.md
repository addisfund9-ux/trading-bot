## Ideas

### 1. If-then plan that the bot shows you after a loss
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Write 2–3 rules ahead of time, like "If I lose a trade, then I close Binance for 15 minutes." Plans set in if-then form have a medium-to-large effect on sticking to goals and on holding back emotional reactions. The bot repeats your rule after each loss.
- **Notes:** Gollwitzer et al. summarise meta-analyses (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006; Webb & Sheeran 2008) that find a medium-to-large effect of implementation intentions beyond simple goal-setting. They also report that these plans reduce emotional arousal and improve stopping responses. Practical points for wording: make the "if" a specific cue ("I close a losing trade", "it is my 8th trade today"), and make the "then" a concrete, easy action ("close the app for 15 minutes", "stop for the day"). Adding a "why" to the plan did not help and sometimes weakened it, so the rule should stay short. Build: a `/plan` command that stores up to 3 if-then lines in store.py; the 08:00 brief prints them; after an import, /tilt shows which rule's "if" fired and whether the next trade broke the "then" (for example, a trade within 15 minutes of a loss). This works with the revenge-trade detection tilt.py already does.

### 2. Fee drag line in /report and /tilt
- **Area:** risk · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Binance charges 0.05% taker fee on the full position size, so a 40× round trip costs about 4% of your margin before price moves (2% at 20×). Show fees paid per day against gross PnL, so overtrading days show what the extra trades cost you.
- **Notes:** Binance's fee page gives VIP 0 USDⓈ-M rates as 0.02% maker and 0.05% taker, charged on notional value (contract size × price). Maths: notional = margin × leverage, so two taker fills cost 0.1% × leverage of margin (10× = 1%, 20× = 2%, 40× = 4%). importer.py already finds a "fee" column in the trade export header, but the value doesn't appear to be stored or used anywhere else. Build: store the fee per trade, then add "Fees: X USDT (Y% of gross)" to /report and the "Fees on trades after the 8th" to the overtrading part of /tilt. If an export has no fee column, estimate it from notional × 0.05% and label it as an estimate.

### 3. "Thin" flag for movers with a wide spread
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Top movers are often thin coins where the spread and slippage eat a scalp. One bookTicker call covers every symbol: flag a coin when its spread is many times BTC's. At 0.25% slippage each way, a trade must move 0.5% just to break even.
- **Notes:** The Buildix guide gives no fixed cutoff. It suggests comparing a perp's spread with BTC/ETH perps (a deep perp's spread is a fraction of a basis point, while a thin one's can be 1% or more), checking resting depth within 1–2% of mid, and treating a fill that moves price well past 1% as too thin for that size. Build: in scanner.py, call `GET /fapi/v1/ticker/bookTicker` once with no symbol (it returns all symbols), compute spread = (ask − bid) / mid, and add a "Thin" flag when it is over a set multiple of BTCUSDT's spread (start with 10×, tune later). Drop Thin coins from `clean_picks()` the same way as other bad flags, and mirror this in webapp/index.html. This is a new endpoint for the bot. Its request weight wasn't confirmed today, so check it in the Binance docs before shipping. Depth-within-1% would need `/fapi/v1/depth` per coin and is a possible later step.
