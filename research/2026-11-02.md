# Daily research — 2026-11-02

**Headline:** No API changes break the bot today; the ideas check whether a red morning makes you trade bigger in the afternoon, measure volume against the same hour on past days, and show your odds of a 50% drawdown from your own trades.

## Ideas

### 1. Red-morning check: does a losing morning make your afternoon bigger?
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** A study of 150 prop day traders found they traded more and bigger after a losing morning, and the ones who did it most earned the least. /week would show your own afternoons after red vs green mornings; after a red morning the guard caps the afternoon at 2 trades and half size.
- **Notes:** Garvey, Murphy & Wu split each day at 12:45 and used 16,260 trader-days (about 730k round trips). After morning losses, afternoon trade count, trade size and dollar volume were all above the trader's average. After gains they were below average. Morning profit had a negative coefficient in 14 of 16 regressions (5% level), and traders with the strongest bias did worst. Build: in tilt.py / review.py, split each day at a fixed Addis time (e.g. 14:00), label the morning red or green, then compare afternoon trade count, median notional, max leverage and PnL in /week. If, after 2+ weeks, red mornings show bigger afternoons, guard.py can apply an afternoon cap (2 trades, half the morning's median notional) when the day is red at the split time. This is different from the 26 Oct step-down idea: that one reacts to each loss, while this one looks at the whole morning and is checked against your own history first.

### 2. Volume spike measured against the same hour on past days
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** The scanner's volume spike compares the last 3 hours with the day before, so the busy hours always look like spikes. Comparing with the same hour over the last 7 days shows which movers have unusual volume right now, not just normal busy-hour volume.
- **Notes:** In scanner.py `analyse()`, `spike = avg(last 3 closed 1h qvol) / avg(the 24 before)`. The 1h klines call already fetches 300 candles (~12 days), so a time-of-day RVOL needs no extra requests: for the last closed hour, divide its quote volume by the mean of the candles 24, 48 … 168 hours earlier. Trade Ideas describes this as the intraday version of RVOL and says it fixes the "busy open always looks big" problem. hours.py already shows that crypto volume has a daily rhythm, so the same bias applies here. Keep the old number for a week next to the new one (e.g. "vol 2.1× · same-hour 1.2×") before changing the score weight. Mirror the change in webapp/index.html.

### 3. Ruin odds from your own trades in /week
- **Area:** risk · **Effort:** medium
- **Why:** Replay your last 30 days of trades, as whole days so tilt streaks stay together, 10,000 times. /week shows your odds of losing half the account over the next 200 trades at your current size, at half size, and with the revenge trades removed.
- **Notes:** NexusFi describes Monte Carlo risk of ruin (resample your trade P&L, count runs that hit the ruin line) and warns that shuffling single trades understates risk when losses cluster. Your losses do cluster (revenge chains), so resample whole days instead (block bootstrap). Inputs come from store.py: each trade's PnL as % of the balance at the time. Output three lines: current, half size, and "calm trades only" (tilt.py's non-revenge rows). The gap between line 1 and line 3 puts the cost of tilt in drawdown terms. Show "not enough data" under ~15 trading days. Their table, for a 55% / 1.5R system, goes from under 0.1% ruin at 1% risk per trade to ~15–25% at 5%. At 20–40× with wide stops your risk per trade can be well above that.
