# Daily research — 2026-11-09

**Headline:** No API changes break the bot today and research was thin, so there are just two ideas, both from streak studies: let a broken clean-day streak be repaired, and show clean days per week so one bad day doesn't wipe the slate.

Focus today: trading psychology and discipline tools (streaks), plus the routine check for API and platform changes.

## Ideas

### 1. Repair a broken clean-day streak
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Studies find a broken streak makes people quit, but a repair option softens that. After a tilted day, the next day can restore your /lessons streak if it is clean and you stop at half your trade cap. One bad day stops being a reason to give up on the rules.
- **Notes:** Silverman & Barasch (Journal of Consumer Research, 2023, seven studies) found that an intact logged streak raises later engagement compared with a broken one. The effect depends only on how the behaviour is shown in the log, not on the behaviour itself. The drop is larger when people blame themselves for the break, and smaller when they can "repair" the streak. Silverman's advice to app makers is not to dwell on "you broke your streak" messages and to give people another way to keep it going. That is the risk after a day like 25 Oct: the /lessons streak (lessons.py `build()`) currently resets to 0 on the first tilted day, which is when the urge to "make it back" is strongest. Possible rule: one repair per 7 days; the repair day must be clean and use at most half of `max_trades`. Show "streak repaired" instead of "streak broken". Days off still don't break the streak, as now.

### 2. Clean days this week next to the streak
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Alongside the streak, /lessons and the 08:00 brief show 'clean days: 4 of last 7' and your best streak. A run of clean days can make you feel safe, so the line also says your caps stay the same however long the streak is.
- **Notes:** In "Hot streak!" (Silverman, Small & Barasch, OBHDP, 2023), people who met a goal three days in a row were more confident they would keep meeting it than people with the same three successes spread across a week. A consecutive run inflates confidence without more evidence. For a 10–40× scalper, that confidence can show up as bigger size. A count over a fixed window ("4 of last 7") is steadier than a streak and doesn't drop to zero after one bad day. `per_day[...]["rings"]["clean"]` in lessons.py already holds what's needed. The "caps stay the same" line is a fixed message, not a new rule.
