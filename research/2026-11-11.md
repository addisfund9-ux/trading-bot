## Ideas

### 1. Soft stop before the daily loss limit
- **Area:** risk · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Today the guard speaks once, when you've given back 5 USDT. Add a first warning at 3 USDT: 'half size, no adding, no new coins', with room left counting your open loss too. You get a nudge while it can still change the next trade.
- **Notes:** NexusFi's daily-loss-limit article sets a soft limit at "typically 50–75% of the daily maximum". Hitting it means half size, no averaging in and no new instruments. Hitting the hard limit means flatten, stop and cool down for at least 30 minutes. It also suggests an alert on *unrealised* loss at 50–60% of the hard limit. The Right Trader's worked example counts room left as cap − closed losses − open risk. Both are rules of thumb; neither page cites a study, and The Right Trader says "There is no reliable published figure for the best daily percent." Build: guard.py already has `loss_limit` and checks `peak - run >= self.loss_limit` (line ~172). Add `soft = 0.6 * loss_limit` with its own `("soft", day)` key in `self.sent`. guard.py already reads positions, so the unrealised PnL from positionRisk can go into the giveback figure: "Given back 3.10 of 5.00 (incl. 0.80 open). Half size, no adds, no new coins." Show the same soft/hard numbers in the 08:00 brief. This differs from the 30 Oct "three losses ends the session" idea (a count of losses) and the 3 Nov "green-day lock" (a profit floor). This one is a money-based early stage on the giveback rule the bot already has.

### 2. Catch size-ups after wins, not just losses
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** tilt.py flags bigger leverage or size only after a loss. 25 Oct started while you were green. Also flag size or leverage going up after 3 wins in a row, and have /tilt show whether your rule breaks cluster during win streaks.
- **Notes:** tilt.py's Escalation check is documented as "after a loss, the next trade uses higher leverage or a much bigger position". NexusFi's winning-streak article proposes fixed risk per trade whatever the recent results. It suggests a streak threshold (3, 5 or 7 wins) that triggers a review and a stricter entry bar, and logging rule violations against current streak length to test the link in your own data. Its evidence is mostly forum posts, so it is a hypothesis to check, not a finding. Lippi et al. (Judgment and Decision Making, 2018) found investors at one Italian bank "become more risk-averse after suffering losses and more risk-seeking after experiencing gains". That was 62 people and monthly portfolio risk, not intraday futures, so it is only suggestive. Build: in `tilt.analyse`, track the current win streak; when the streak is ≥3 and the next trade has `lev_up` or `size_up`, label it "Streak size-up". /tilt adds one line: "Rule breaks during a 3+ win streak: X of Y". This differs from the 7 Nov "5-minute gap after every trade" idea, which is about timing; this one is about size.

### 3. Limit days add up: hit it twice, next week trades smaller
- **Area:** risk · **Effort:** medium
- **Why:** /week counts days you hit the loss limit. Twice in a week and the Monday brief sets size 25–50% lower; three times and the week starts with the bot's paper picks only. One bad day costs a day, a bad week costs some size.
- **Notes:** NexusFi suggests weekly and monthly caps (about 3× and 6× the daily one, with no roll-over of unused allowance). Its escalation rule: hit the daily limit twice in a week and cut size 25–50% the next week; three or more times and pause live trading. These are the article's own rules, not tested figures. TradeZero's circuit-breaker piece makes the same point about setting the limit before the session from start-of-day capital: "Once the maximum is reached, trading stops for the day." It is a broker blog written by a paid author. Build: tilt.py already computes `past_limit` per trade, so a limit day is any day with a `past_limit` row. /week lists them. A small table in store.py keeps `size_factor` for the week (1.0 / 0.6 / paper). The Monday 08:00 brief prints it, and guard.py can compare position notional against it. "Paper only" points to the bot-picks paper journal that already exists (/picks), so the routine keeps going without real money. Leave the override to Dagi but make it explicit: the brief asks him to type a reason to lift it.
