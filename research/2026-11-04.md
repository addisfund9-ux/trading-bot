## Ideas

### 1. Tilt alerts that lead with what you keep
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** In a 2024 UCLA study, people chose the patient option more often when the reward was shown before the wait. Reword cooldown alerts the same way: "Keep your +6.20 today. Next entry at 14:32" instead of "15 min cooldown". It's a wording change in tilt.py and guard.py.
- **Notes:** Chen, Zheng, Wang & Krajbich (Nature Communications, 2024) found that when participants looked at the reward first they more often picked the larger, later option. When they looked at the delay first, they went for the smaller, sooner one. Their conclusion: "Immediately focusing on the benefits of waiting might help people improve their self-control." This was a lab study of money-now-vs-later choices, not trading, so treat it as a cheap experiment. Build: in guard.py's cooldown/loss-limit messages, put the protected amount first (today's PnL if green, or "your remaining daily budget: X USDT" if red), then the time of the next allowed entry as a clock time rather than a countdown. Keep the same text in /tilt. After 2–3 weeks, /tilt can compare how often an entry followed within 15 minutes of an alert, before and after the change.

### 2. Stop distance from your own winners' worst dip
- **Area:** risk · **Effort:** medium
- **Why:** For each imported trade, use klines to measure how far it went against you before closing. /week then says "95% of your winners never dipped more than 0.6%". A stop just past that cuts losers early without killing winners, and shows when you held a loser far past that line.
- **Notes:** This is Maximum Adverse Excursion (MAE). The NexusFi guide's method: collect trades, histogram the winners' MAE, find where it drops off (e.g. 95% of winners stay inside X), and place the stop a little beyond it. It suggests 200+ trades and redoing it as volatility changes. Live data is better than backtests because it includes slippage and late entries. Build: tradechart.py already pulls klines for a trade window. Add a function that, for each position from store.py, fetches 1m klines from open to close and records MAE and MFE as % of entry (price move, not leveraged ROE). /week shows the 90th/95th percentile winner MAE, the median loser MAE, and how many losers went past the winner line and were still held. Also show the leveraged view: at 20× a 0.6% move is 12% of margin, and liquidation sits about 5% away. With fewer than ~50 trades, print "not enough trades yet" rather than a number. 1m klines cost little weight, but cache them in SQLite so /week doesn't refetch.
- Related: Garvey & Murphy (2004) found that even profitable prop day traders held losers longer than winners (about 268 vs 166 seconds) and would have done better cutting losers sooner. review.py's "losers held over 10 min" check catches time; MAE adds distance.

### 3. "Late" flag from the age of the EMA alignment
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Each scanner card shows how many 15m bars since EMA 9 crossed 21, and 1h bars since 20 crossed 50. A top mover whose alignment is 40+ bars (10h) old gets a "Late" flag, so you can tell a fresh trend from one that has already run while you slept.
- **Notes:** TradeStation's "Bars Since MA Cross" indicator on TradingView counts bars since a fast MA crossed a slow one. It "judges a move by its age and not its intensity" and notes that crosses tend to reverse after a while. The scanner already has a "Stretched" flag (distance from the EMAs). This adds time, which is different: a coin can be close to its EMAs but have trended for 12 hours. Build: scanner.py and webapp/index.html already compute the EMA series from klines. Walk back to find the last sign change of (ema9 − ema21) on 15m and (ema20 − ema50) on 1h, and show "15m 6 bars · 1h 3 bars". Flag "Late" above a setting (default 40 × 15m bars). Don't change the score at first. Use the evening scorecard idea from 3 Nov, or backtest.py, to check whether Late setups do worse before giving it weight. Keep the Python and web versions identical.
