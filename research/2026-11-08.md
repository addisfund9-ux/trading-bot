## Ideas

### 1. Time stop: a clock on every scalp
- **Area:** risk · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Pick a hold limit before entry, e.g. 4 candles on 15m (1 hour). If neither stop nor target has hit, close it. /report lists trades held past the limit and what they made, so you see whether slow trades are where your losses grow.
- **Notes:** The Right Trader defines a time stop as a bar count fixed before entry and tied to the chart you trade; don't reset the count when price nearly moves, and record the real R you booked (e.g. -0.5R, not "flat"). It is an addition to the price stop, never a replacement, and the limit should not be stretched on leveraged trades. A crypto-bot backtest on DEV (author's own numbers, no fees/out-of-sample shown, promotes a product) found a hard stop plus a time exit beat either alone. Build: store.py already has opened_at/closed_at; review.py can bucket hold time in 15m candles and show P&L for trades over the limit. Make the limit a setting and print it in the 08:00 brief next to the leverage/size plan.

### 2. Tag why you closed each trade
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** After each imported trade, one tap: Plan, Fear (early exit), FOMO entry, or Moved stop. /week shows P&L per tag. After ~30 trades you see in numbers what breaking your own rules costs, beyond revenge timing.
- **Notes:** JournalPlus's discipline guide uses exactly these four exit codes and says about 30 tagged trades are needed before patterns show. Their worked example (illustrative) found FOMO entries averaged -0.8R while rule-following trades averaged +0.3R. This complements the entry-reason tag idea from 1 Nov: entry tag = why you went in, exit tag = whether you followed the plan on the way out. tilt.py catches timing-based tilt (revenge, flips, escalation); exit tags catch what the timestamps can't, like moving a stop. Inline keyboard buttons on the import confirmation message; store the tag in a new column.

### 3. "Wait a candle" flag after a taker-driven spike
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** A 2026 study of 183 Binance pairs found 15-minute moves tend to partly reverse, most after aggressive taker buying or selling. Flag movers whose last 15m candle was big and mostly one-sided takers: wait for the next close before entering.
- **Notes:** Kitron & Wengrowicz (arXiv 2608.21888) compared 183 Binance pairs with 187 US stocks/ETFs out of sample: ~90% of Binance pairs show significant 15-minute reversal versus 2.7% of US names, and the effect grows with taker-flow intensity. The edge is tiny (~1.3 bp gross vs ~5 bp costs), so this is NOT a reason to fade moves — only a reason not to chase the close of a spike candle at 20–40× leverage. Build: klines already include taker buy base volume (field 9); flag when the last closed 15m candle's range is > ~2× the 20-candle average and taker-buy share is > ~70% (or < ~30% for shorts). Show it next to Stretched in scanner.py and webapp/index.html.
