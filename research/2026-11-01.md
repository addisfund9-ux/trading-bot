## Ideas

### 1. Tag why you took each trade
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** After each import, the bot asks one tap per trade: Plan / Chased / Revenge / Bored. /week then shows PnL by tag. tilt.py spots revenge by timing; this adds your own honest reason, so you see exactly what 'Chased' and 'Bored' trades cost you.
- **Notes:** TradesViz describes tags mainly as a filter for review: mistake tags such as "Revenge Trade" and "Emotional Trading" let you count how often emotion drove a trade and what it cost, and setup tags let you compare win rates. It also notes that tags carry little context, so a one-line note can be optional. Build: after an inbox import, send each new closed position (coin, side, leverage, PnL) with an InlineKeyboardMarkup of 4 buttons; a CallbackQueryHandler stores (position id, tag) in a new store.py table. Batch them into one message per import to avoid spamming 15 buttons, and let untagged trades stay untagged rather than guessing. In /week: count, net PnL and win rate per tag, plus "tilt.py called it revenge but you tagged Plan: N" — the gap between the two is itself useful. Different from the 29 Oct morning mood check (per day, before trading) and the 31 Oct on-list/off-list split (automatic, by coin).

### 2. Total exposure warning in the tilt guard
- **Area:** risk · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Alts tend to fall together when the market drops, so 3 alt longs at 20× are really one big bet. The guard already reads your open positions: add up their size and the loss if all hit their stops together, and warn when it passes your daily limit.
- **Notes:** Gkillas, Bekiros & Siriopoulos (2018) find that correlation between the large cryptocurrencies rises significantly in the tails, and "extreme correlation increases in bear markets, but not in bull markets". Ahn (2022) likewise finds crypto co-movement is much stronger in downturns than upturns, so a symmetric "they're only loosely correlated" view understates the risk. guard.py already pulls `/fapi/v2/positionRisk` every 20 seconds and computes `notional` per position, but nothing adds them up. Build: each poll, sum notional and margin across open positions, split by direction. Alert when (a) 2+ positions are open in the same direction ("3 longs = one 1,450 USDT bet on the market going up"), or (b) the sum of losses at each position's stop (if a stop order exists; otherwise at an assumed 1% move) exceeds the daily loss limit or the 5 USDT give-back threshold. Rate-limit the alert to once per new position. The bot places nothing; it only reports.

### 3. "Liq burst" flag on scanner cards
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** medium
- **Why:** Some top movers are being pushed by forced liquidations, not new buyers, and the book is thin while it happens. Binance's free liquidation stream can show 'shorts liquidated: 180k USDT in 15 min' on a card, so you don't enter in the middle of forced flow.
- **Notes:** Binance's `!forceOrder@arr` WebSocket stream covers all USDⓈ-M symbols and pushes symbol, side, price, average price and filled quantity — but only the latest liquidation per symbol per 1000 ms, so treat totals as a lower bound. A 2026 study of a cascade (Garcia Seuma, arXiv 2608.03616) found 88% of post-onset forced selling came within 30 minutes, with price impact spiking while OI cleared 25–70%. A study of Hyperliquid after the 10 Oct 2025 cascade (Lim, JFDA 2026) found spreads recovered within the day but order-book depth stayed at 66–79% of pre-event levels for weeks. Neither paper measures whether price reverses afterwards, so the flag means "forced flow, thin book" — not "it will reverse". Build: the Mini App is only open briefly, so the bot (running on the PC) is the better listener: a small asyncio task in bot.py keeps the socket open, keeps a rolling 15-minute total per symbol and side (buy-side liquidations = shorts squeezed), and exposes it to scanner.py. Flag "Liq burst" when 15-minute liquidations are large next to the coin's normal 15-minute volume (e.g. over 5% of 24h quote volume ÷ 96 — a starting guess to tune), and show it next to the existing "OI falling" flag. Check the current WebSocket base URL in the Binance docs before building; the bot currently uses REST only. CoinGlass links already exist on cards for manual checking.
