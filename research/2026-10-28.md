## Ideas

### 1. Three losses in a row ends the session
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** tilt.py counts revenge trades but not loss streaks. Add a rule: 3 losses in a row means you're done for the session, and 3 red days in a row means a day off, then half size. On 25 Oct this would have stopped you after the third loss, before most of the 9 revenge trades.
- **Notes:** The NexusFi protocol suggests stopping the session after about 3 consecutive full stop-outs (4 for low win-rate systems), or stepping down to 50% size after 2 losses. At the weekly level, it suggests taking one day off after 3 net-negative days in a row and restarting at 25–50% size. A "loss" should mean a trade closed at a loss, not a scratch. Breakeven trades could be ignored, for example anything within ±0.05 USDT. The article's maths: at a 50% win rate a 3-loss streak happens in about 12.5% of sessions, so the stop won't fire every day. The article is practitioner guidance and cites no controlled study of these exact thresholds, so treat 3 as a starting point to tune. Build: in `tilt.analyse()`, keep a running loss-streak counter and mark `after_streak` on every trade opened after the streak hit 3. Add a group for it, like `over_count` and `past_limit`, so /tilt shows "Trades after 3-loss stop: N, PnL X". In `week_report()`, count red days in a row and print "Day off suggested" in the 08:00 brief the morning after the third. This differs from the 26 Oct step-down cap, which cuts leverage; this one stops trading.

### 2. "Unusual for you" alert from your own baseline
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** medium
- **Why:** A study of 150 day traders found that after morning losses they traded more and bigger in the afternoon, and the ones who did this most earned less. Compare each trade's leverage, size and trades-per-hour with your own 20-day median and flag anything far above it.
- **Notes:** Garvey, Murphy & Wu studied 150 proprietary day traders (Jun 2002–May 2003, about 730k round trips). After a morning loss, afternoon trade count, trade size and the size of price moves traded were all above average. Morning profit was negatively related to afternoon risk in 14 of 16 regressions (significant at 5%). Traders with the strongest bias underperformed. The authors suggest firms use automated alerts that flag departures from a trader's normal risk-taking. Fixed limits (8 trades, 2× size) don't adapt to you, but a personal baseline does. Build: store.py already holds past trades. For each import, compute the 20-day median of leverage, notional per trade and trades per rolling hour. Flag a trade when it is over 1.5× the median notional, above the usual leverage, or when the hourly count is over 2× its median. Show "Unusual for you: 4 trades" in /tilt and in the daily report. Medium effort because it needs a baseline query and some tuning. It needs at least 10 trading days of history before it turns on.

### 3. "Absorbed" flag when price rises but sellers dominate
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** The 1h klines the scanner already downloads include taker buy volume. If price went up over the last 4 hours but net taker volume was negative on above-average volume, someone is selling into the move. Flag it and keep it out of Clean picks, with no extra API calls.
- **Notes:** The open-source "CVD Multi Exchange Percentile" TradingView script defines bearish absorption as price rising while CVD falls (and bullish as the reverse). It only counts this when volume is above 1.5× its 10-bar average, and the author calls these signals context, not standalone triggers. CVD is the running sum of taker buy minus taker sell volume. Binance's USDⓈ-M kline rows carry "Taker buy quote asset volume" at index 10, next to quote volume at index 7, which `klines()` already reads. Build: in `scanner.klines()`, also keep `tbq = float(x[10])`. Over the last 4 closed 1h bars, delta = Σ(2·tbq − qvol). If `d * ch4 > 0` but `d * delta < 0`, and the 4-bar volume is over 1.5× the earlier average, add an "Absorbed" flag and add it to `clean_picks()`'s bad set. Mirror the same in webapp/index.html. The existing takerlongshortRatio score (3 × 1h) can stay. This adds a clear flag tied to price direction and volume, rather than another small score adjustment.
