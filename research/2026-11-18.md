## Ideas

### 1. "Too thin for your size" check on the top picks
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** medium
- **Why:** At 20–40× your position is large for a small coin. For the 5 morning picks, read the order book and work out how far price would slip if you filled your usual size. If that slippage eats a big share of a normal scalp, the card says so before you enter.
- **Notes:** This is the "later step" left open by the 27 Oct "Thin" (spread) idea. A spread check only covers the best bid and ask; at high leverage the problem is your own notional. Buildix: "If filling your size pushes price well past 1 percent from mid, the pair is too thin for that size", and a pair that costs 0.25% each way needs about 0.5% just to break even. Coin Bureau says the same thing for spot: there is "no fixed volume ... threshold", so size against your own order. Build: in scanner.py, only for the candidates that reach `clean_picks()` (to keep request weight low), call `GET /fapi/v1/depth?symbol=X&limit=50`, walk the asks (for a long) and bids (for a short) for his typical notional (median notional of the last 50 imported trades from store.py, or a setting), and compute the average fill price vs mid. Flag "Thin for size" when one-way slippage is above about 0.1% or above a quarter of his median winning move; show the number on the card ("fill 0.14% from mid"). New endpoint for the bot: check its request weight in the Binance docs before shipping. The Mini App can't know his size, so either pass it as a URL parameter from the bot or keep this flag bot-side (/scan, /morning) only.

### 2. Who is moving it: 15-minute OI + taker label
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** The scanner reads OI and taker flow on 1h, but you trade the 15m. Label each card "new longs", "shorts closing", "new shorts" or "longs closing" from the last 15m, so you can tell a move with fresh money behind it from a squeeze that may already be done.
- **Notes:** AnomIQ: OI is "direction-neutral on its own"; taker flow supplies the direction. Rising OI with buy-side taker flow = new longs; falling OI with buy-side flow = shorts closing ("possible squeeze or stop-out"). Their example 15m thresholds: OI change above +1.0% / below −1.0%. They say themselves that they "haven't tested whether it leads to a larger move", so treat this as a label to read, not a score input, at first. Build: scanner.py already calls `openInterestHist` and `takerlongshortRatio` with `period="1h"`; add a second call with `period="15m", limit=3` for the same two endpoints (or switch the taker call to 15m), compute OI change over the last 15m and whether buySellRatio is above or below 1, and add the label next to the existing "OI falling" flag. Mirror in webapp/index.html. After two weeks, /week could split his results by label to see if "shorts closing" entries do worse for him. Differs from the 31 Oct "Absorbed" flag (price up while sellers dominate) and the 1h "OI falling" flag: same data, but on his entry timeframe and with all four cases named.

### 3. 20-second pause on the scanner during a cooldown
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** After a loss, opening the scanner to find the next trade is the first step of a revenge run. While the tilt guard's cooldown is on, the scanner first shows today's P&L and your next allowed entry time for 20 seconds, then shows the coins.
- **Notes:** Evidence is anecdotal, not a controlled study: Kanebridge/WSJ reports a crypto fund manager who added a 20-second delay before opening trading apps and checks them "60% less", and that use of the One Sec delay app for trading and banking apps more than quintupled from 2021 to 2022. The same piece cites a 2021 Indiana University study (Kalda) where day traders took more frequent, riskier bets on phones than on desktop. Build: when guard.py starts a cooldown, the bot's "📈 Open scanner" button (bot.py, `WebAppInfo(WEBAPP_URL)`) gets `?cool=<unix end time>&pnl=<today>`; the chat menu button is set once with `set_chat_menu_button`, so also re-set it with the parameter when a cooldown starts and back to the plain URL when it ends. In webapp/index.html, if `cool` is in the future, show a full-screen card ("Today: +x.xx USDT · next entry 14:32") with a 20-second countdown before rendering the list. No server needed; it fits the Telegram 10.2 same-origin rule because it's the same Netlify page. Count how often he waits out the 20 seconds vs closes the app.
