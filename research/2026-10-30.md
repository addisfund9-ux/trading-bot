## Ideas

### 1. Tilt alert offers Binance's cooling-off lock
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Binance Futures has a cooling-off period (1 day up to a month) that can't be lifted once set. When the bot sees a revenge pattern or a 5 USDT give-back, it should tell you to switch it on now. A lock you can't undo works when willpower doesn't, like on 25 Oct.
- **Notes:** Binance's Responsible Trading page says that for Futures and Margin, "Once the Cooling-off Period is enabled, it cannot be lifted and will only terminate after the preset cut-off time." CoinCheckup describes the choice as one day to one month, reached from the upper-right corner of the Futures homepage (that article is older, so check the current options in the app). The bot has no API key and cannot turn it on for you, so this is a nudge: when tilt.py's checks fire (revenge trade, flip, leverage escalation, giving back 5 USDT, over 8 trades), the message ends with "Lock futures for today: Futures → top-right menu → Cooling-off → 1 day" plus a button that logs whether you did it. /week can then count "locks taken vs tilt days". This is stronger than the 27 Oct if-then plan or the 28 Oct 3-loss stop, because the exchange enforces it rather than you. Write the exact menu path into the message only after you've checked it once on your phone.

### 2. Results by hour of day and hours into the session
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** In a trading experiment, people trading at their less alert times took riskier positions and earned about a third less. Split your PnL and rule breaks by Addis hour and by hours since your first trade, so the brief can say when you should already be done for the day.
- **Notes:** Dickinson, Chaudhuri & Greenaway-McGrevy ran an asset-market game at six times of day (4am to midnight). Players trading at their less alert times bought at inflated prices and held longer, and in mixed markets tired players earned about a third less on average. These were students in a lab game with small stakes, so treat it as a reason to check your own data, not proof. Build: in tilt.py / review.py, group closed trades by local hour (UTC+3) and by "hours since first trade of the day" (0–1, 1–2, 2–4, 4+). Show net PnL, win rate, count and revenge trades for each bucket in /week, and with 2+ weeks of data add a line to the 08:00 brief such as "Your PnL turns negative after 3h of trading: stop by ~13:00 if you start at 10:00." On 25 Oct the gains came early and the giving back came later in the session, which is the pattern this would make visible.

### 3. "Choppy" flag from the 15m Choppiness Index
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** EMA 9/21 alignment often flips back and forth in sideways chop, which is where scalps bleed. The Choppiness Index (14 bars) runs on the 15m candles the scanner already has: over 61.8 means sideways. Flag those coins and keep them out of Clean picks, with no extra API calls.
- **Notes:** TradingView's definition: CHOP = 100 × log10( Σ ATR(1) over n / (highest high(n) − lowest low(n)) ) / log10(n), default n = 14. Readings above 61.8 suggest continued sideways movement and below 38.2 a continuing trend. Build: scanner.py already fetches 15m klines with high/low/close. Compute true range per bar for the last 14 closed bars, sum it, divide by the 14-bar range, and apply the formula. Add a "Choppy" flag when CHOP > 61.8 and put it in `clean_picks()`'s bad set; mirror the same in webapp/index.html. A top mover can be choppy on 15m after the main move is done, which is exactly when late entries get chopped up. The 61.8 cutoff is convention, not a tested optimum, so log CHOP next to outcomes for a couple of weeks before tuning.
