## Ideas

### 1. Green-day lock once you're well up
- **Area:** discipline · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** Floor traders take more risk after gains (the house money effect). Once the day is up 5 USDT, the guard keeps at least half the day's high, allows 2 more trades and no leverage above the morning's. On 25 Oct that would have locked in about +4 instead of 9 revenge trades.
- **Notes:** Frino, Grant & Johnstone (2008) found professional "locals" on the Sydney Futures Exchange trade in a way consistent with the house money effect (more risk after gains), and found no significant loss aversion. The NexusFi article suggests keeping risk per trade fixed whatever the streak, raising the bar for setups after a run of wins, and cutting size 25–50% after big gains. Yesterday's note pointed to a lab study (Ackert et al., 2003) where the effect was weak, so the threshold should be tuned on your own days. Build: guard.py already tracks `run` and `peak` in `day_pnl()`. Add a `GREEN_LOCK` setting (default 5 USDT). Once `peak >= GREEN_LOCK`, switch the day to "lock" mode: the floor is `peak * 0.5` (it rises with the peak), and the guard alerts on more than 2 new positions or leverage above the day's first trade. tilt.py can mark the same rows (`after_lock`) so /tilt shows "Trades after green lock: N, PnL X". This differs from the existing fixed 5 USDT give-back limit: it ratchets with the peak and also caps trades and leverage while you're ahead.

### 2. Big-loser check: median trade vs total
- **Area:** risk · **Effort:** small
- **Why:** In Taiwan futures, the median day trade made money but the average lost, because a few big losses outweighed many small wins. /week shows your median trade and what your 5 worst trades cost, and the guard warns when an open loss passes 2× your usual loss.
- **Notes:** Kuo & Lin studied about 348,000 trades by 3,470 individual day traders on the Taiwan Futures Exchange (Oct 2007–Sep 2008). The median net profit per trade was +189 TWD, but the mean was −613 TWD, and fewer than 20% of traders made a net profit. That is the "many small wins, a few big losses" shape, which high leverage makes worse. review.py already prints avg win vs avg loss. Add the median trade, the share of total losses from the worst 5 (or worst 10%) of trades, and "PnL without your 5 worst". In guard.py, compute the median losing trade over the last 30 days and send one alert per position when unrealized PnL from `positionRisk` passes 2× that. It complements the existing `no_stop` and `hold` checks, which look at stop orders and time, not loss size.

### 3. Evening scorecard for the morning list
- **Area:** scanner · **Effort:** medium
- **Why:** The 21:00 reminder shows how the 08:00 brief's top 5 coins moved over the next 4 hours against the scanner's own 1.5 ATR stop and 1.5R target. After 2–4 weeks you can see whether the list and its Crowded/Stretched flags actually help, from live data rather than the backtest.
- **Notes:** backtest.py replays Clean signals over the last ~30 days, but it can't show how the list you actually saw each morning played out. Build: at 08:00, save the top 5 (symbol, side implied by EMA alignment, score, flags, price, 1h ATR) to a `morning_picks` table in store.py. At 21:00, fetch 15m klines from 08:00 to 12:00 for each and record max favourable and adverse move in R, and whether stop or target came first (stop wins a tie, as in backtest.py). /week then shows hit rate by flag (for example "Stretched: 1 of 6 reached target"). This tests the scanner, not the trader, so it gives no trade calls. It only shows whether the scoring is worth trusting.
